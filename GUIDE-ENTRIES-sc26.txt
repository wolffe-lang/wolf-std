## 2026-08-28 (sc26) — the native lane comes home; a ledger survives a platform

- A recorded ledger is portable: lighting the native + wolfc lanes on
  macOS/arm64 for the first time (s59-apple, the un-parked
  libwolf_rt.a) moved 1 of 365 rows — 364 linux-lane values silently
  confirmed on the new platform, zero divergences, the whole crypto
  tier included. The predicted env-crypto-drift class produced
  NOTHING. When a lane lights on a new platform, run the untouched
  ledger FIRST and expect agreement; every difference must earn its
  row the way this one did.
- The one mover got a mechanism, not a mystery, and the old binary is
  the tool: `testing/near_and_ulps` wolfc flipped `unsupported` ->
  `run` at c1ca543, and rebuilding the SUPERSEDED sha's driver (one
  checkout + one -p build) captured the dying refusal verbatim —
  `this literal shape in checked execution` spanning order_key's
  INT_MIN literal, s123's numeric-literal wave the owner. When a bump
  moves a row you did not predict, the old pin is a five-minute build
  away; spend it.
- wolf `--version` still names no pin (pre-s66), so a bump's
  acquisition is verified BEHAVIORALLY: pick a behavior the span
  guarantees and a stale binary cannot fake. sc26's witness is s125's
  two-line trap report — `wolf run` on an oob index prints
  `wolf-trap: bounds` then `  at file:line:col` (exit 134) where the
  old binary prints one line. Cheap, decisive, and it doubles as the
  feature's own smoke test. The rig itself never sees the second line
  (it reads conform-run verdicts), which is why zero rows moved on
  s125 — tolerance by architecture beats tolerance by patch.
- Native wall clocks on this rig are NOT the gauntlet's cost: all 160
  native-run x/ rows, timed one at a time idle, total 79s with the
  slowest at 1.40s against the 60s ceiling (43x margin — zero
  knife-edge rows on the lane that just lit). What is slow is the
  checked tier grinding to its step budgets and lupin grinding to its
  50M-step refusals: a three-lane 365-row gauntlet is ~50 min idle
  here. Rig facts, recorded as rig facts.
- A registry regen delta deserves the drift discipline too: the
  contract predicted +2 anchors, the measured delta was +5/-1, and the
  -1 (gram.lex.ident) was an upstream extractor bug — s126's shebang
  prose added bare literal `[` runs and spec-extract's scanner pairs
  each `[` with the NEXT `]`, swallowing a still-normative anchor's
  only occurrence (F-0100 / wolf-lang#170, filed same-day). Diff the
  anchor SET at every re-vendor and explain every removal; "the number
  went up by about the right amount" would have hidden this.
- A zero-cost-when-absent claim is verifiable by the letter: s126's
  D61 marker cost this corpus nothing, and two greps prove it rather
  than trust it (`#![` nowhere in std/ or tests/; no `#!`-initial
  line anywhere). When upstream says "zero cost when absent", grep for
  the absence.
- The park/un-park pattern worked end to end: a hazard discovered at
  provisioning (a lit native lane on a pre-s59 binary) was parked with
  a dated comment naming its exit (s59-apple), and the exit arrived
  the same day the record said it would — bootstrap.sh's park case
  died with its dates attached. A workaround that names its own
  retirement condition retires on schedule; one that does not becomes
  architecture.
