## 2026-09-02 (sc33) — the bytes get a width

- A BUMP CAN CHANGE WHAT A RECORD *SAYS* WITHOUT CHANGING WHAT ANY
  LANE *CONCLUDES*, and you find out which by reading the comparator
  rather than the release notes. lupin 0.1.23 put trap-path stdout in
  its records (#55). Eleven std rows trap after printing, so eleven
  records changed shape — and zero rows moved, because this rig never
  looks at a trap's stdout in three separate places: `classify`'s Trap
  arm discards the field BY PATTERN, `diff_class` compares
  `stdout_sha256` only under `Verdict::Exit(_)`, and lint R3 bars
  `stdout=` beside a trap expectation outright. The right prediction
  was not "eleven movers" and not "zero movers" — it was both, at two
  different levels, and the only way to know was to open the rig and
  read what it compares. A release note tells you what an
  implementation now EMITS; only your own rig tells you what you now
  CONCLUDE. Predict at both levels or you will report the wrong number
  in one direction or the other.

- MEASURE BOTH SIDES OF A BUMP WHEN THE CLAIM IS ABOUT THE BUMP. The
  #55 before-picture was taken at 0.1.22 with the old binary still
  installed, and it is what turned a dull restatement of the changelog
  into the actual finding: both wolf lanes ALREADY carried trap stdout,
  with the byte-identical digest lupin would later produce, so the
  asymmetry was lupin's alone and it had been invisible for exactly the
  reason it is harmless — the comparator does not look. Five minutes
  spent before the fresh-inode install bought a fact that cannot be
  recovered afterwards at any price.

- THERE ARE THREE KINDS OF ZERO AND THE THIRD IS THE CHEAPEST: NAME
  WHICH ONE YOU HAVE. sc31's empty drift list came from a span with no
  new capability. sc32's came from a capability with no carrier — a
  sprint's worth of work sitting in front of the lane. sc33's wolfc
  zero is neither: **the binary did not move at all**, because the
  sprint took a lupin release and a data-pin advance while wolf stayed
  at its tag. All three print as "0 movers" and they cost completely
  different amounts to defend. Say which one you have in the first
  sentence: half the drift work evaporates honestly, and the half that
  remains gets the attention it actually needed.

- A PIN IS THREE PINS — TELL THEM APART BEFORE YOU CLAIM AN INVARIANT,
  AND PREDICT FROM THE GATE'S SOURCE RATHER THAN THE INVARIANT'S PROSE.
  This sprint suspended the "data pin follows the binary pin" one-sha
  invariant deliberately, leaving the data pin nineteen commits ahead
  of both binaries. Whether that reds anything is answerable from
  source in two minutes: doctor NEVER READS `vendor/upstream/PIN` — it
  gates the binary's self-declared version and pin against
  `vendor/tools.toml` — and `sync-pin` gates the snapshot against the
  SUBMODULE, not against either binary. So the answer was "green and
  silent, by design", predicted in writing and confirmed. An invariant
  recorded in a comment is a CONVENTION; only a gate is a RULE, and
  the difference is one grep.

- WHEN THE FIX YOU ARE ASKING FOR HAS NO CLAUSE TO SIT BESIDE, SAY
  *THAT* FIRST. The most useful sentence in the #203 proposal is not
  the ask. It is that the spec has no type inventory at all: twenty
  `[type.*]` anchors in three families, no `[type.int]`, no width
  vocabulary, no literal suffixes, and `int` itself carrying no
  defining clause anywhere. Going to look for the clause you would
  AMEND, before writing the ask, is what turns "please add a `u8`"
  into "this is the first clause of an inventory that does not exist"
  — a different piece of work, correctly scoped, and a different
  conversation with the lane that owns it. Find the hole's edges
  before you describe the hole.

- TWO CHEAP ANSWERS THAT FAIL BY THE SAME MECHANISM ARE WORTH FAR MORE
  THAN ONE THAT FAILS. sc32 MEASURED that a std `struct Bytes { xs:
  List[int] }` changes no allocation. sc33 READ that the spec's own
  newtype is no better: `[gram.item.trait]` defines `distinct` as
  "same layout as the base", because layout preservation is the whole
  point of the construct. The library-side workaround and the
  language-side newtype fail identically — one by measurement, one off
  the page — and setting them side by side closes the "can't you just
  …" question from both directions at once. A single refutation invites
  a variation; a matched pair names the mechanism and ends the thread.

- THE PRECEDENT YOU NEED MAY ALREADY BE IN THE DOCUMENT, ONE TIER OVER.
  `[type.char]` is the spec's ONLY clause that gives a scalar a stated
  width, and it carries every part a byte type would need: a domain, a
  layout, a `List[T]` stride, a C-seam rule, a no-arithmetic posture
  and an explicit cast bridge. `[mem.str.chars]` even says why the byte
  tier was left behind — "`char` is the scalar tier, never a byte". So
  the proposal became "finish the job s121 started" rather than "invent
  a primitive". Search the spec for the SHAPE of your answer before you
  design one: a request modelled on a clause the document already
  accepted is far harder to refuse, and it inherits every argument that
  clause already won.

- RE-MEASURE THE COUNTS YOU ARE ABOUT TO QUOTE, EVEN OUT OF YOUR OWN
  FINDINGS FILE. F-0104 said "all nine functions in `std.bytes`"; at
  this pin there are ten. Nothing depended on the number, which is
  precisely why it survived — and it was two keystrokes from being
  restated in a public upstream issue as this repo's own evidence. A
  figure nobody re-checks is a figure quietly rotting toward the first
  place it will embarrass you.
