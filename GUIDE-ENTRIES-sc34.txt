## 2026-09-02 (sc34) — the byte tier is bytes, and it cannot be yet

- A TYPE LANDING IS NOT THE SAME EVENT AS ITS PRODUCERS LANDING, AND A
  SPRINT WHOSE JOB IS "SUBSTITUTE THE NEW TYPE" SHOULD CHECK WHICH ONE
  HAPPENED BEFORE IT EDITS ANYTHING. `[type.byte]` shipped complete —
  clause, casts, operators, both backends, the checked machine, three
  witnesses — and every builtin that PRODUCES or CONSUMES a byte
  sequence stayed declared over `List[int]`. Sixteen std signatures sit
  on eight such builtins as thin wrappers, so "rename the type" turned
  into "insert a conversion at every boundary", which a cumulative
  ledger prices at 17x/18x against the 16x it replaces. The check is
  one grep of the callee side's signature table, it takes two minutes,
  and it is the difference between a rename and a regression. Read the
  table, not the release note.

- WHEN THE PLAN SAYS "A PURE SUBSTITUTION", THE WORD TO AUDIT IS
  "PURE", AND THE AUDIT IS A MEASUREMENT. Three documents said this
  change was a rename: the decision, the sprint contract, and the
  upstream issue's own closing comment. All three were right about the
  SHAPE — the signatures really are monomorphic and really do keep
  their form — and all three were describing a pipe that did not exist
  yet. The measurement that settled it took one program and four
  numbers. If a plan's central claim is quantitative, spend the twenty
  minutes to produce the quantity before you spend the day on the
  edit; the plan is a hypothesis with a good pedigree, not a result.

- A SUBSTITUTION THAT WINS ON ONE TIER AND LOSES ON ANOTHER IS NOT A
  PARTIAL WIN, IT IS A BET, AND A THREE-LANE LIBRARY DOES NOT SHIP
  BETS. The one shape with a real gain — building a `List[byte]` by
  walking a borrowed byte view — read 131,120 natively (a clean 8x) and
  1,114,112 checked. Same source, same pin, opposite verdicts about
  whether the change is an improvement. The instinct to take the win
  "where it works" is how a library acquires a per-tier performance
  cliff nobody documents. Take the tier-uniform win or take none, and
  file the reason the tiers disagree — which in this case turned out to
  be a bug worth more than the substitution would have been.

- MEASURING THE THING YOU DECIDED NOT TO BUILD IS THE CHEAPEST
  EVIDENCE YOU WILL EVER PRODUCE. The fourth column of this sprint's
  table — "what a substituted reader would charge" — is four lines of
  probe and it is the whole argument. It converts "we judged this
  premature" into "it costs 18x where the status quo costs 16x, here
  are seven sizes", which is a sentence an upstream maintainer can act
  on and a reviewer cannot argue with. Whenever you decline a change,
  build the smallest possible model of it and price it. A declined
  change with a number attached is a filed issue; a declined change
  without one is an opinion.

- A LEDGER BUG HIDES BEST WHERE THE CLAUSE FORBIDS TESTING IT. No
  conforming test in this tree may print a `region_bytes` count,
  because the units are per-tier and the clause says so — which is
  correct, and which also means an entire class of divergence is
  structurally invisible to every gauntlet and every differ. The
  checked machine charging 16x for a walk that allocates nothing on the
  shipping tier was found by a probe written for another purpose
  entirely. When a clause tells you a value is not comparison surface,
  read that as "nothing will ever check this for me" and probe it by
  hand at every bump.

- A PROBE THAT REPRODUCES THE SYMPTOM IS NOT YET A PROBE THAT PINS THE
  TRIGGER, AND THE DIFFERENCE SHOWS UP AS A FALSE HEAL. Three sprints
  described F-0103 as "an unhandled raising call passed straight into a
  row-typed parameter". A minimal file of exactly that shape RUNS on
  the checked lane — because the callee ignored its parameter, so no
  control flow was lowered for the argument. The refusal needs the row
  to be CONSUMED in the callee; the trigger is a pair, and the recorded
  description named half of it. Had this sprint stopped at the first
  green it would have reported a heal that had not happened. When a
  re-probe suddenly passes after several pins of refusing, suspect the
  probe before the compiler.

- THE FOURTH KIND OF ZERO IS THE EXPENSIVE ONE: A BINARY THAT MOVED
  FURTHER THAN ANY BEFORE IT. sc31's empty drift list came from a span
  with no capability, sc32's from a capability with no carrier, sc33's
  from a binary that did not move at all. sc34's came from 51 commits
  of new compiler, every carrier of which had to be grepped for by
  name: the record extension keys against the record parser's key list,
  the parse-span change against what `classify` compares, the path
  spelling against whether the rig runs a package verb at all, the
  reset-socket fix against the one `set_deadline` call site in the
  tree. Same printed result, four very different costs. Say in the
  first sentence which kind you have — and when it is the fourth, budget
  for it, because the cheap answer is not available.

- A BINARY'S TAG AND A BINARY'S CAPABILITY ARE DIFFERENT QUESTIONS.
  The contract said "the machine wolf stays at v0.2.3", and the v0.2.3
  TAG turned out to sit twelve commits before the feature the sprint
  existed to use — its lowering refuses the type by name. Both
  sentences are true at once: the machine stays 0.2.3 and the tag build
  cannot do the job, because a dev stamp keeps the version and moves
  the pin. The lesson is to ask the binary rather than the version
  string: one four-line program answered in a second what no amount of
  reading the release notes would have settled, and it was the last
  moment that answer was obtainable, since the binary was about to be
  replaced.
